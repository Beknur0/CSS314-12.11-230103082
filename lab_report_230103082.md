### Analysis

At stride 8, the distance between slots was 64 bytes, matching the
measured cache-line size. Median time fell from 2060.444 ms at stride 4
to 1742.124 ms at stride 8, a decrease of approximately 15.4%.

For p = 2, the Karp–Flatt metric was 0.5518 at stride 1 and 0.1244
at stride 16. This is consistent with additional contention when
workers write to the same cache line.

However, e(p) did not increase monotonically. Process startup,
scheduling, simultaneous multithreading and measurement variability
can also affect the result. At p = 12, e(p) was slightly higher for
stride 16, so the data does not demonstrate a steady increase in
e(p) for stride 1.

### Analysis

At p = 1, the measured Stage A fraction was 0.016544. Amdahl's
model therefore predicts a maximum speedup of 60.4463 and a speedup
of 10.1525 at 12 workers. The observed speedup at 12 workers was
5.1822, below the model prediction.

Weak-scaling efficiency decreased from 1.0000 at p = 1 to 0.4415
at p = 12. In this experiment the serial Stage A also grows with N,
so its growing cost contributes to the decline.

The marginal strong-scaling speedup was 0.094623 from p = 5 to p = 6,
and 0.295350 from p = 6 to p = 7. Thus, there was no measured drop
at this particular boundary: the reported -212.13% drop corresponds
to an increase. The curve is noisy, and these measurements alone
do not establish a clear SMT inflection.

Estimated ridge point:
0.258605 / 14.649 = 0.01765 FLOP/byte.

The copy bandwidth uses an assumed traffic volume of one read and
one write per byte copied; it is not a direct DRAM traffic measurement.
The compute result measures this Python loop, not the hardware FMA peak.
Both measurements showed substantial variability.
